What counts as a reliable answer?

Under the university QA situation, defining what should count as a reliable answer is important.

We propose four criteria that both the model and the retriever need to meet: Evidence-based, Summarize instead of copied, Citation useful, and No blocking case.

Limits of standard RAG evaluation

At first, we used RAGAS as a mainstream evaluation method to evaluate the whole-system RAG performance. However, after running it across our six models, we found that the score differences were very small.

Therefore, our conclusion was that the current RAGAS evaluation framework has certain constraints and does not properly cover the dimensions that we care about, especially readability, model generation consistency, and blocking-case detection.

G-Eval-inspired answer-quality evaluation

Because we wanted to expand our evaluation metrics into dimensions that we care about for the university QA use case, we customized G-Eval's evaluation framework into dimensions that fit our own needs.

The judges gave scores from 0 to 2 on four dimensions: answer acceptance, readability, citation usefulness, and over-answering risk.

Working reliability requirement

As we evaluate model output by assigning scores from 0 to 2 on each evaluation dimension and then normalize the final quality score, we use 0.8 as a practical bottom line to filter out models that are obviously not capable enough for this task.

Therefore, in our definition, a workable model condition needs to meet the 0.8 quality threshold and also have zero blocking cases.

Blocking cases

In this thesis, we define a blocking case as an answer where answer acceptance is zero from any of the two judges, or the average human quality score is below 0.50, or the judge notes/tags indicate repetitive or truncated output.

Such blocking cases can significantly affect the user's decision and experience. Therefore, they should be carefully examined inside the output and avoided when building the system.
