ICU Wiki

A case study in reliable local RAG for university question answering.

This thesis uses ICU Wiki, a locally hostable RAG-based chatbot for university question answering, as a case study to find out how we should define and identify a local configuration that is small enough but reliable for similar tasks, including both the language model and the retrieval-and-orchestration layer around it.

Main finding

The main finding is that under ICU's university corpus, Japanese tokenizer led to better Japanese-language handling, source-authority scoring made the retriever select evidence from the correct source category, and reranking reduced the number of blocking cases.

Meanwhile, average score alone is not sufficient for clarifying the reliability threshold that we can use to select the final model candidates.

Research narrative

From scattered university information to a reliability decision.

01 / Problem

University QA is an evidence problem.

University information is often fragmented across multiple websites and hidden behind layers of menus, making it difficult for students to find expected answers efficiently.

Open corpus section

02 / System

The system is more than a language model.

ICU Wiki is built as a local RAG system with three main parts: a cleaned ICU corpus, a retriever that selects top evidence chunks, and an answer generator based on a local language model.

Open interactive retriever

03 / Evaluation

Average scores were not enough.

The judges gave scores from 0 to 2 on four dimensions: answer acceptance, readability, citation usefulness, and over-answering risk.

Open evaluation section

04 / Decision

Reliability was decided with blocking cases.

After marking blocking cases across these model/mode conditions, we find that Gemma 4 E4B agentic mode and Qwen3.5 9B agentic mode are the only two conditions that are above the quality threshold while also having zero blocking cases.
